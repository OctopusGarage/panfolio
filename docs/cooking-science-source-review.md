## Evidence reviewed

The supplied Bilibili videos are by 石墨橙:

- https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1474w6XEp5/ — frying, roasting and flavour, approximately 17 minutes.

Complete video and audio files were downloaded locally. Both complete audio tracks were transcribed with local Whisper large-v3-turbo. Minute-spaced contact sheets were visually inspected to check the presentation and explanatory diagrams; this was sampled frame review, not frame-by-frame viewing. These videos prompted a review of the existing collection. No corresponding original YouTube uploads were established. Their explanations must not be attributed to the chefs whose recipes are associated below. Reader-facing YouTube links identify concrete preparation examples already in the collection, not the origin of every scientific explanation. This record retains the Bilibili provenance and independent explanatory checks.

## Independent checks and editorial synthesis

- [IFST: gelatinisation](https://www.ifst.org/lovefoodlovescience/resources/carbohydrates-gelatinisation): starch swelling and thickening in heated liquid. The slurry reference combines that mechanism with the existing braised-aubergine method. Stirring settled slurry and avoiding localised addition are editorial operational guidance, not a newly claimed quotation from that recipe video.
- [Exploratorium: braising](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/cooking/icooks/2-1-03article.html): connective tissue softening and muscle firming can occur together. The braising reference applies this distinction to the existing tomato beef recipe without inventing a new cooking time or safety temperature.
- [King Arthur: dough spring-back](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2024/02/14/how-to-stretch-pizza-dough): relaxation helps shaping. This supports the general explanation only; the pancake reference keeps its own partially scalded dough method and staged rests, not pizza fermentation instructions.
- [Cooked-rice retrogradation research](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jhej1951/37/8/37_8_667/_article/-char/en): cooling-related starch changes support the qualitative rice-texture explanation. No nutrition, storage lifetime or absolute irreversible-temperature claim was adopted.

The reduction reference is an editorial comparison of the existing tomato-beef covered braise, aubergine sauce and gently reduced steak cream sauce. It preserves early seasoning and does not impose high-heat reduction on dairy sauces.

## Content decisions

Added four bilingual references: slurry thickening, covered cooking versus reduction and final seasoning, braise tenderness, and resting pancake dough. Each has actionable steps, common errors, applicability limits, genuine recipe associations and bilingual search aliases. Existing recipe instructions remain intact; related-reference links are derived from reference recipe IDs.

Expanded eight existing references: drying before searing, cold/hot pan choice, pan contact and turning, fried rice, chicken thickness, garlic oil, onions and roasted cauliflower. Retained cold-pan chicken, the measured chicken-centre check, the short-grain rice water addition, garlic timing alternatives and existing recipe-specific quantities.

Rejected universal temperature thresholds for browning, droplet tests for all cookware, metal pores closing with heat, fixed salt penetration rates, a universal thickness-to-time calculator, searing as waterproof sealing, mandatory overnight rice, universal late salting, generic bicarbonate/enzyme dosing, blanket oil smoke-point rankings and simplified umami multiplication claims. No safety or storage procedure was inferred from the videos. No new recipe, cover or taxonomy was created because the material is explanatory and the ice-flower dumpling example lacks a complete reproducible recipe.

## Review limits

This is source-informed editorial synthesis, not experimental validation of every mechanism. Scientific explanations are deliberately qualitative. Platform captions and any on-screen-only information outside the sampled frames remain unverified. Reader-facing text contains useful limitations rather than extraction commentary.

## Second-pass additions

A second pass adds two references and targeted explanations without expanding the recipe catalogue:

- `starch-coating-or-slurry` compares thin meat coatings, thicker batter and sauce thickening. Concrete examples come from the existing pork-chop, rice-cooker meal and aubergine recipes. The comparison does not prescribe a new deep-frying method, universal ratio, egg-white requirement or bicarbonate addition. [Rouxbe’s velveting guidance](https://marquise.rouxbe.com/tips-techniques/608-velveting-mixtures-for-stir-fries) supports variation in coatings.
- `bare-cast-iron-care` covers routine care of seasoned bare cast iron only. [Lodge’s current cleaning instructions](https://www.lodgecastiron.com/pages/how-to-clean) support hand washing, drying and a thin oil film; [its myth guide](https://www.lodgecastiron.com/pages/cast-iron-101-cast-iron-myths) addresses soap and acid exposure. The reader-facing video is the actual Lodge video embedded in its cleaning page: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y-3W2A1W7bE. The page text and embed identity were reviewed; full video playback was not reviewed. An older Lodge PDF URL returned 404 and was not used as current evidence. No unrelated recipe is associated merely because it could be cooked in cast iron.
- The broccoli reference now combines colour with texture checks and cautions against adding bicarbonate solely for colour. [FAO’s vegetable chemistry discussion](https://www.fao.org/4/v5030e/V5030E06.htm) supports the trade-off between pigment retention and softening. The original broccoli timing and scope remain intact; spinach and water-spinach recipes were not linked to that timing.
- Tomato beef and rice-cooker meal tips describe umami without new ingredients or multiplication claims. [Umami Information Center FAQs](https://www.umamiinfo.com/faqs/) distinguish glutamate, meat inosinate and dried-mushroom guanylate. Fresh mushrooms are not assigned the same guanylate profile as dried shiitake. These are editorial explanations, not claims that the recipe creators made those statements.
- Pan-contact and shrimp-egg references distinguish switching off the heat, leaving a hot pan and completing cooking. The chicken recipe explicitly transfers the cooked thigh to a plate before resting. Existing measured centre-temperature checks and recipe-specific residual-heat cooking are retained; no estimated carryover increase replaces them.

Clear/white stock, bean softening, pressure cooking, enzyme tenderising and a new dumpling recipe remain deferred for lack of a sufficiently complete, relevant preparation in the present collection. No new artwork is needed for this reference and text-only work.

## Final targeted pass

The final pass keeps the reference count at 37. It clarifies gentle bubbling versus vigorous boiling in `reduce-and-season`, preserving low-heat cream reduction and the existing recipe targets. The [UC Agriculture and Natural Resources explanation of boiling states](https://ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2023-09/388651.pdf) supports observing the liquid rather than copying a burner setting.

Chicken thigh, chicken breast and pork-chop tips now distinguish following their prescribed marinade from extending it indefinitely or adding acid, enzymes or bicarbonate. Refrigerated marinating is independently supported by [USDA food-safety guidance](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety). No storage duration is inferred from the videos.

The existing lamb pilaf is a concrete bicarbonate preparation, so its specified quantity, short marinating, rinse and dry sequence remain intact. The added explanation distinguishes that treatment from salt and starch coating, and does not generalise the recipe to other meats. [America’s Test Kitchen’s bicarbonate treatment discussion](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how-tos/6707-tenderizing-meat-with-a-baking-soda-solution) supports its distinct purpose. No new dose or conversion is introduced.

Steamed fish and ribs gain brief notes tying their existing times to thickness, quantity, arrangement and steam supply. These are qualitative applicability boundaries, not new timings or a complete food-safety validation. No universal steaming temperature, doneness calculator or additional reference is created.

## Pre-publication review corrections

Independent review identified a reader-visible attribution gap: method videos alone could be mistaken for evidence of every synthesized explanation. Affected references now label those links as related videos and link this evidence record explicitly. The same distinction and record URL are exported in canonical Markdown and AI JSON, so MCP and packaged consumers retain the provenance distinction. Existing references without editorial synthesis retain their original source labels.

The dough reference now includes the original staged kneading and resting times instead of requiring readers to recover them from the linked recipe. Broccoli texture checking is placed before chilling and draining. The English braising cut is corrected to rib fingers to match the linked recipe. Appended recipe tips have consistent list spacing. These corrections change presentation and completeness, not source quantities, timings or safety thresholds.
