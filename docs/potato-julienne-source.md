## Editorial decisions

Preserve the demonstrated grip: ring and little fingers on the handle, thumb and middle finger pinching the blade, index finger assisting. The English caption's “little thumb” means little finger, not another thumb. Keep the thin Chinese slicing cleaver context and the vertical blade in both cutting stages. Do not replace the specific potato-spacer technique with an unsupported generic anti-stick claim.

Slices are spread and overlapped flat before cutting strips. The advanced demonstration overlaps two prepared portions and mentions still larger batches; the reference retains two portions for the optional batch step without encouraging beginners to stack as many as possible. No strip thickness or speed target is invented. The square, even cross-section is the useful quality cue rather than requiring readers to imitate the presenter's finger-spinning flourish.

The source says freshly cut strips go into water to reduce discoloration, and repeated rinsing removes surface starch until water is clear. Its demonstration mentions more than ten washes, but clarity is retained as the endpoint rather than imposing a universal wash count. It also asserts a 24-hour preservation result without establishing household storage conditions. That fixed holding period is not imported into the reference. The entry concerns preparation and pre-cooking treatment, not a validated long-term storage schedule.
