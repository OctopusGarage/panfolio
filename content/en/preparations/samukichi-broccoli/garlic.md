# Garlic broccoli

Japanese chef series · Preparation type: dish

ID: samukichi-broccoli / garlic

Use the selected ingredients and method below. Shared tips apply only when their food and conditions match this preparation.

## Ingredients

- Broccoli (florets and peeled stalk)
- 1 teaspoon salt for blanching
- Ice water
- Garlic version: olive oil, garlic, salt, a little soy sauce and black pepper

## Method

### 1. Wash upside down

Submerge the florets upside down in a bowl of water and swirl gently to loosen trapped debris. You can also cover with paper towels for a brief soak.



### 2. Cut evenly

Score the branching stems and separate by hand into similar-sized florets. Lightly peel tough outer layers. Trim the tough end of the main stalk, peel and cut it for cooking too.



### 3. Blanch briefly

Bring water to a boil and add 1 teaspoon salt. Blanch the broccoli for 40–60 seconds, watching for a vibrant green color. 



### 4. Chill and drain

Transfer immediately to ice water to stop residual cooking. Drain and spread out in a colander to remove excess moisture before seasoning.



### 5. Garlic version: sear

Slowly heat olive oil and garlic in a pan until fragrant. Start with the broccoli stems against the pan and sear over medium to medium-high heat until lightly browned. Season with salt, add a little soy sauce around the pan edge, then turn off the heat and finish with black pepper.

## Tips

- Debris can lodge between florets, so a quick rinse may not be enough. Submerge the crown upside down and swirl gently to loosen trapped particles.
- Uneven florets cook unevenly: smaller pieces soften before larger ones. Score the stems and separate by hand for similar sizes and a natural shape.
- The stalk is edible too. Trim the tough base and peel its coarse skin to reduce toughness and enjoy a different sweetness and texture from the florets.
- Brief salted blanching brings out sweetness. Prolonged vigorous boiling dulls the color, makes the texture watery and can intensify bitterness; water-soluble nutrients also enter the cooking water.
- Residual heat keeps cooking broccoli left standing after blanching. Chill immediately, then drain thoroughly to retain a crisp bite and avoid diluting the seasoning.
- For the garlic version, infuse the oil slowly before lightly browning the broccoli. A little soy sauce around the pan edge adds a roasted aroma; add black pepper off the heat to preserve its fragrance.
- Use a thin coating of miso with the savory cheese and richness of olive oil. Red and yellow bell peppers add color and texture.
- After the basic preparation, choose either the garlic or miso-cheese version. Steaming is another way to prepare broccoli while preserving its sweetness.
