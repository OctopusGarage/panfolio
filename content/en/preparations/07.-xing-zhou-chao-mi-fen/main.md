# Singapore-style fried rice vermicelli

Chinese · Preparation type: dish

ID: 07.-xing-zhou-chao-mi-fen / main

Use the selected ingredients and method below. Shared tips apply only when their food and conditions match this preparation.

## Ingredients

- 150 g rice vermicelli and about 100 g char siu pork
- Salted shrimp, 2 eggs and about 100 g bean sprouts
- 30–50 g yellow garlic chives, green and red peppers, onion, congtou (aromatic allium bulbs), scallion whites and greens
- 20 g oil-based curry paste, 5 g turmeric and 10 g Guilin chili sauce
- 15 g water or cooking wine, for loosening the sauce
- Salt and cooking oil

## Method

### 1. Prepare the noodles

Add 3–5 g salt and 8–10 g oil to boiling water. Add the noodles and cook for 1–2 minutes until slightly softened. Drain and cover so residual heat softens them further.

### 2. Prepare the other ingredients

Blanch the salted shrimp for about 10 seconds. Blanch the char siu to remove surface sugar, then slice. Cut the peppers and onion into strips, cut the scallions and chives into lengths, and beat the eggs.

### 3. Mix the curry sauce

Mix the curry paste, turmeric and Guilin chili sauce. If too thick, add 15 g water or cooking wine until it flows.

### 4. Cook the additions separately

Heat about 10 g oil and scramble the eggs, then remove. Stir-fry the shrimp over high heat for about half a minute and remove. Add about 5 g oil, fry the onion, then add the peppers, 2 g salt and bean sprouts. Fry until slightly softened, then spread out to cool.

### 5. Fry the noodles

Heat 5–6 g oil. Spread out the noodles, let them fry briefly, then turn them and fry until dry, fragrant and lightly toasted. Cut very long strands with a spatula if needed.

### 6. Add the sauce and other ingredients

Turn off the heat and mix in the curry sauce. Turn the heat back on and fry until fragrant. Add the congtou bulbs and cooked additions, including the char siu, and toss quickly.

### 7. Finish and serve

Add the scallion lengths and yellow garlic chives. Let residual heat cook them, then serve.

## Tips

- The noodles do not need to be fully cooked during the initial boiling.
- Lower the heat if the pan starts to turn white and smoke, to avoid burning the noodles.
- Blanching the char siu first helps prevent its sugary surface from sticking.
