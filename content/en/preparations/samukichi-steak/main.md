# Pan-seared steak with mustard cream sauce

Japanese chef series · Preparation type: dish

ID: samukichi-steak / main

Use the selected ingredients and method below. Shared tips apply only when their food and conditions match this preparation.

## Ingredients

- 1 sirloin steak, about 250 g and 2.5～3 cm thick
- Salt and cooking oil as needed
- About 5 g unsalted butter for the steak
- 30 ml white wine or water
- 40 ml heavy cream
- 10 g whole-grain mustard
- 5 g unsalted butter for the sauce
- Black pepper and salt to taste; a little water to adjust the sauce

## Method

### 1. Dry and season

Thoroughly pat both sides dry with paper towels. Salt evenly just before cooking and rub it in gently. Alternatively, salt ahead, place on a rack uncovered and refrigerate for at least 45 minutes. Check the surface before cooking and remove excess moisture.

### 2. Brown the edges and broad sides

Preheat the pan, coat with oil and add about 5 g butter. Hold the steak upright to brown the fatty edge, briefly sear the opposite edge, then cook the broad sides. Start around medium heat and adjust to the pan and meat. Turn when the first side is nicely browned and releases relatively easily, then brown the other side. The butter should be fragrant and golden brown; reduce the heat immediately if it smells burnt or produces heavy smoke.

### 3. Lower the heat to cook the center

Once both sides are browned, lower the heat and turn roughly every 30～60 seconds to warm the center gradually. Briefly remove the pan from the heat if the crust gets too dark. Check the center with a food thermometer; remove when it reaches at least 63°C and rest for at least 3 minutes. Do not leave the steak in the hot pan after switching off the heat.

### 4. Make the pan sauce

While the steak rests, pour off excess fat and remove blackened residue, keeping the flavorful brown bits. Return to low heat, add 30 ml white wine or water and scrape the bottom. If using wine, reduce by about half to mellow its sharp aroma. Add 40 ml cream and gently thicken over low heat.

### 5. Finish off the heat

Turn off the heat and stir in 10 g whole-grain mustard and 5 g butter. Add black pepper, taste and adjust the salt. Add a little water if the sauce is too thick or strong. Slice the rested steak and serve with the sauce.

## Tips

- A wet surface uses heat to evaporate moisture, delaying browning. Too little heat also prolongs browning and can overcook the center; keeping the same heat after browning can toughen the outside. Control browning and center cooking as separate stages.
- The video's texture references are about 57°C for medium-rare, 63°C for medium and 71°C for well-done; these describe doneness preferences. USDA safety guidance for whole beef steaks is at least 63°C internally followed by at least 3 minutes of rest. A lower preference target does not meet that guidance. Color, touch and crust cannot replace a thermometer.
- This preparation uses a little butter at the start for flavor, so watch it for burning. The sauce butter is stirred in off the heat; these are different uses with different timing.
- Resting away from the pan avoids its continuing intense heat. Do not leave the meat in the original hot pan to rest.
