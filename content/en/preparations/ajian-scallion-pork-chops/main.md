# Ajian’s scallion-salt pork chops

Chinese · Preparation type: dish

ID: ajian-scallion-pork-chops / main

Use the selected ingredients and method below. Shared tips apply only when their food and conditions match this preparation.

## Ingredients

- Thin pork collar slices, about one and a half jin
- Oyster sauce, light soy sauce, white pepper and water
- Two large handfuls of sweet potato starch and cooking oil
- A large handful of garlic and a handful of scallions
- One small spoonful each of salt, sugar and pepper
- One large spoonful of white sesame seeds and sesame oil

## Method

### 1. Marinate and coat

Tenderize or pierce both sides of the pork collar slices with a knife spine or fork. Massage with oyster sauce, light soy sauce, white pepper and water; marinate for 10 minutes. Mix in sweet potato starch until the meat carries a thin batter coating.

### 2. Mix scallion seasoning

Crush and mince garlic; finely chop scallions and place both in a bowl. Thoroughly mix with salt, sugar, pepper, white sesame seeds and sesame oil.

### 3. Pan-fry over medium-low heat

Heat the pan, add oil and wait until the oil is hot before adding pork. Fry slowly over medium-low heat. Turn once the underside has set, then turn several more times until thoroughly cooked and golden and fragrant on both sides. Remove.

### 4. Cut hot and return to the pan

Cut the pork into small pieces while hot and return to the pan. Add the scallion seasoning and toss over low heat until the garlic and scallion are fragrant and coat the meat; serve.

## Tips

- Thin slices take seasoning and cook more readily; tenderize or pierce both sides before marinating. Black pepper may replace white pepper.
- Do not rush large chops over maximum heat: the coating can burn before the inside cooks. Add meat to hot oil to help reduce sticking and let the underside set before turning.
- Cut and eat while hot; the coating loses crispness as it cools. This is a savory scallion-garlic seasoning that also contains a little sugar.
- Marinate the thin slices as directed before applying the thin coating; longer marinating does not necessarily mean greater tenderness. Do not automatically add baking soda or an acidic treatment. Refrigerate during marinating; cooking through still depends on thickness and actual heating.
