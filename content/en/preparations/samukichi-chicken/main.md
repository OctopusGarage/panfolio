# Cold-pan crispy chicken thigh

Japanese chef series · Preparation type: dish

ID: samukichi-chicken / main

Use the selected ingredients and method below. Shared tips apply only when their food and conditions match this preparation.

## Ingredients

- 1 boneless, skin-on chicken thigh, about 150 g (serves 1)
- 1.2–1.5 g salt, about 0.8–1% of the chicken weight
- 20 g olive oil and 1/2 clove garlic for marinating
- Optional: 5–10 g butter, a little soy sauce, 1 lemon wedge and a little black pepper

## Method

### 1. Trim and dry

Remove firm fat, sinew and cartilage while retaining some soft fat. Open the thick areas with shallow cuts from the meat side without cutting through the skin. Even out the thickness and pat both sides dry.

### 2. Season and marinate

Salt both sides evenly, place in a container and coat with the olive oil and garlic. Allow about 30 minutes if short on time; preferably marinate in the refrigerator for 6 hours to overnight. Wipe excess moisture and oil from both sides just before cooking.

### 3. Start in a cold pan

Place skin-side down in a cold pan, then turn on medium-low heat. Cook mainly in the rendered chicken fat; lightly oil the pan if the chicken is lean or the pan tends to stick.

### 4. Keep the skin in contact

Press gently with a spatula for the first 30 seconds to 1 minute, especially where the center lifts. Alternatively, place parchment on the meat side and use a light weight to maintain contact. Remove the weight and paper once the skin sets; do not crush the meat.

### 5. Render slowly and turn once

Keep medium-low heat and leave the chicken undisturbed until it releases naturally, the skin is evenly golden and the sides turn white. Flip once and lower the heat. As a guide, spend about 80% of cooking time on the skin side and 20% on the meat side. Do not press after turning.

### 6. Baste and check doneness

When nearly cooked, optionally melt butter in an empty part of the pan. Tilt the pan and spoon it mainly over the flesh and edges to preserve the crispy skin. Insert a thermometer from the side and ensure the center reaches at least 74°C before turning off the heat.

### 7. Season and rest

Off the heat, optionally add a little soy sauce and lemon to the pan butter, with black pepper if desired. Transfer the chicken to a plate and rest skin-side up for 3–5 minutes before cutting. Serve the sauce beside the chicken to keep the skin crisp; pouring it over the skin softens it.

## Tips

- Package juices can steam the skin and contribute unwanted odor. Drying after trimming and again after marinating helps the skin crisp.
- Remove firm fat, sinew and cartilage but keep soft fat for flavor. Open thick areas from the flesh side to even the thickness, so thin areas do not overcook while thick areas remain underdone.
- Marinating in olive oil and garlic reduces the characteristic chicken odor and adds flavor.
- A hot pan can make the skin contract and lift, causing uneven browning. Starting cold over medium-low heat slowly renders the fat underneath, thinning and crisping the skin.
- Gentle pressure keeps the skin against the pan; it should not flatten the meat. Release the pressure once the skin sets and wait for natural release before turning to avoid tearing.
- Crispness comes from slowly rendering fat, not scorching over high heat. Repeated flipping and sustained high heat make doneness harder to control. Lower the heat after turning and do not press out the juices.
- Add butter only when the chicken is nearly cooked to reduce burning. Baste mainly the flesh and edges. Sauce softens crispy skin; serve it beside the chicken to preserve the crunch.
- Golden skin does not prove the center is cooked; measure the internal temperature. Rest skin-side up before cutting to let the juices settle and keep the skin crisp.
- The hot pan continues heating the contact surface after the heat is turned off. Complete the centre-temperature check, then remove and rest skin-side up as directed; carryover heat is not a reason to skip checking doneness.
- Keep to this recipe’s salt quantity and marinating time rather than extending the time to compensate for uneven thickness or adding acid or tenderising enzymes. Refrigerate while marinating, dry the surface before searing and still check the centre temperature.
