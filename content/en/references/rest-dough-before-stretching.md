# Resting dough that springs back

Complete the meat-pancake recipe’s staged rests before gently stretching, rather than forcing a thin sheet.

## Scope

Shaping the partially scalded dough for layered scallion meat pancakes.

## Steps

1. Mix water and oil into the partially scalded dough as directed, knead for 3–5 minutes, rest for 20 minutes, then knead for another 3–5 minutes until smooth.
2. Portion into an oiled container, brush the surfaces with oil and rest for 60 minutes before gently stretching by hand.
3. If the dough springs back strongly, let it relax further before gently spreading it. After filling and coiling, rest for another 15 minutes before flattening and frying.

## Reasons

- Hydration and relaxation take time. Well-rested dough stretches more easily, while forcing it can leave uneven thickness or tears.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping all resting stages and repeatedly kneading or pressing to overcome spring-back.
- Applying one resting time to every dough while ignoring scalding, hydration and shaping goals.

## Limits

Keep the staged rests in this partially scalded dough recipe; this is not a universal method for all scalded doughs, yeasted doughs or dumpling wrappers.
