# When cooked meat is not yet tender

Choose an appropriate cut and check tenderness: surface browning, cooking the centre and softening connective tissue are different goals.

## Scope

Moist-heat braises such as tomato beef rib fingers, compared with briefly cooked steak.

## Steps

1. Choose a suitable braising cut such as beef rib fingers, dry it and brown in batches. Browning builds flavour rather than proving tenderness.
2. Add the recipe liquid, cover and braise over low heat, keeping liquid in the pot. Do not replace every meat-cooking method with a quick sear.
3. At the recipe checking time, test a piece for easy cutting and tenderness. If still tough, continue gentle braising while monitoring the liquid, then check again.

## Reasons

- Connective-tissue-rich meat needs time to soften in moist heat. Muscle fibres firming and collagen gradually changing can happen together, so cooked does not necessarily mean braise-tender.

## Common mistakes

- Applying the short cooking approach used for steak to every braising cut.
- Relying only on colour or a fixed time without checking the cut, liquid and actual tenderness.

## Limits

This is a texture check, not a food-safety temperature standard. Lean and connective-tissue-rich cuts need different treatment, and recipe times are not guarantees for every size.
