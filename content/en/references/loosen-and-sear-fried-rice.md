# Loosen fried rice, then let it contact the pan

Loosen short-grain rice with a little water, then alternate pan contact and mixing to cook the moisture off.

## Scope

A single portion of fried rice using cooled or slightly surface-dried Japanese short-grain, Calrose or sushi rice.

## Steps

1. Prepare the rice and finely cut additions ahead. Preheat the pan, add oil and lightly cook the egg until it begins to set.
2. Add 200 g cooked rice and immediately break it apart, then add 30 g water to loosen clumps.
3. Start around medium heat and increase as needed. Spread the rice so grains contact the pan. Once the surface dries and becomes aromatic, mix to bring upper grains down, then repeat spreading and mixing.
4. Once the rice is loose and excess surface moisture is gone, add garlic and moderate amounts of relatively dry vegetables or fully cooked meat, then season. Do not add raw meat as a quick-cooking finishing ingredient.

## Reasons

- A little water helps separate clumps and must then evaporate. It is not a reason to keep adding water to already wet rice.
- Constant movement reduces pan contact. Alternating spreading and mixing helps drying and toasted aroma without requiring pan tossing.
- Too many additions introduce moisture and lower the pan temperature, making the rice heavy and wet.

## Common mistakes

- Turning whole clumps without separating their centers.
- Adding water and constantly stirring without allowing hot-pan contact to dry the rice.
- Premixing raw egg and rice while otherwise following this method.

## Limits

The water quantity applies to this cooked short-grain rice, not automatically to wet rice, long-grain rice or arbitrarily larger batches. Other fried-rice methods that premix raw egg should be treated separately.
