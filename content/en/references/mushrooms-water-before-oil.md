# Release mushroom moisture before browning in oil

Start thick white mushrooms with a little water, evaporate the liquid, then brown in oil to avoid using too much oil at the start.

## Scope

Thick pieces of white button mushroom browned in a pan and finished with garlic butter and soy sauce.

## Steps

1. Quickly wash or wipe mushrooms with a damp paper towel, dry thoroughly and cut into thick quarters.
2. Put mushrooms in the pan without oil. This preparation uses 3 large mushrooms and 10 ml water; heat on high until they release moisture.
3. Continue heating to evaporate the liquid. When moisture is almost gone and simmering changes to sizzling, confirm excess liquid has cooked off, then add a little olive oil and reduce to medium-high.
4. Spread out and leave until the contact surface is deeply golden before turning. Lightly salt after both sides brown.
5. Reduce to medium-low before infusing butter with garlic, then toss. Let soy sauce briefly contact the hot pan before coating the mushrooms and turning off the heat.

## Reasons

- Starting with plenty of oil can lead to excess absorption and oil use. Separating moisture release from browning lets a little oil brown the mushrooms after liquid has cooked off.
- Constant stirring limits pan contact. Save butter and garlic for a lower-heat finish after browning to avoid burned garlic and loss of butter aroma.

## Common mistakes

- Long soaking, excess surface water or oil added while liquid remains make browning harder.
- Treating the initial water as a simmering method throughout and skipping evaporation followed by browning.

## Limits

This staged method is for thick white button mushrooms, not a rule to add water to every vegetable. Evaporating liquid is the transition to browning.
