# Lifting shrimp veins and rinsing gently

Check shrimp size and the back vein, lift gently through a shell gap, then rinse, drain and blot before frying.

## Scope

Larger shrimp with noticeable veins, including shrimp prepared for garlic-oil cooking.

## Steps

1. Check the back first: devein larger shrimp with noticeable veins; avoid damaging tiny shrimp simply to remove a fine vein. Remove sharp shell points that could prick your fingers.
2. Find a shell gap near the second or third segment behind the head and bend gently to open it. Slide a toothpick or bamboo skewer beneath the vein, lift, then support it with your fingers and raise it slowly upward without tugging.
3. Rinse gently under running water, drain immediately in a colander and blot with paper towels. Do not substitute prolonged soaking or repeated rubbing.

## Reasons

- Bending opens the gap and reduces catching and breakage; lifting gently damages the flesh less than tugging.
- Long soaking makes shrimp watery; rubbing or salt-scrubbing can wash away flavor.

## Common mistakes

- Tugging hard by hand, breaking the vein and damaging the flesh.
- Insisting on deveining every tiny shrimp and damaging its appearance.

## Limits

Very small shrimp have fine veins and need not always be deveined. This gentle-rinse method is specific guidance rather than the sole washing method for every shrimp dish.
