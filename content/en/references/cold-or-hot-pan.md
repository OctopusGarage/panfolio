# Choosing a cold or preheated pan

Start thighs cold over medium-low heat to release skin fat gradually; preheat for salmon and asparagus instead.

## Scope

Comparing pan-seared skin-on thighs, salmon and asparagus.

## Steps

1. Place chicken skin-side down in a cold pan, then use medium-low heat to render fat slowly.
2. For salmon, preheat and oil the pan, then sear skin-side down over medium heat.
3. Dry asparagus, preheat the oil and sear over medium heat, turning occasionally.

## Reasons

- A hot pan can make the skin contract and lift, causing uneven browning. Starting cold over medium-low heat slowly renders the fat underneath, thinning and crisping the skin.
- Preheating and coating the pan with oil help reduce sticking. Start skin-on fish on its skin side, gently pressing lifted areas only until the skin sets.
- Wet asparagus steams instead of browning; a cold pan lets moisture escape before searing. Dry the surface and preheat the oil for better aroma.

## Common mistakes

- Treating either a hot-pan start or a cold-pan start as a rule for every food.

## Limits

The cold-pan thigh method and preheated salmon method have different conditions and are not interchangeable defaults.
