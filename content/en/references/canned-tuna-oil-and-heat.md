# Oil-packed tuna: keeping oil and timing the addition

Keep some oil with oil-packed tuna and add it near the end of hot dishes; fold into softly set eggs to avoid drying it out.

## Scope

Soft eggs, toast and fried rice made with oil-packed canned tuna.

## Steps

1. Drain excess oil gently while retaining some packing oil; tuna fried rice can use the oil too.
2. Add tuna near the end of hot dishes and warm briefly; fold it into eggs when they are softly set.
3. Balance mayonnaise with a little lemon juice or vinegar. When using tomatoes, salt and blot away moisture first.

## Reasons

- Packing oil carries tuna aroma and savory flavor; draining all of it reduces richness.
- Prolonged heating dries and crumbles the fish; cooking it with eggs from the start also risks tough eggs.
- A little acidity lightens mayonnaise's aftertaste; excess tomato moisture dilutes the assembled dish.

## Common mistakes

- Squeezing oil-packed tuna completely dry.
- Continuing to fry tuna for a long time or adding it as soon as raw eggs enter the pan.

## Limits

Oil retention applies to oil-packed tuna; match heating to the dish. This reference covers cooking rather than storage after opening.
