# Blanching, chilling and draining broccoli

Chill broccoli in ice water as soon as it turns vivid green, then spread and drain it to limit further softening and diluted seasoning.

## Scope

Basic broccoli preparation before the garlic or miso-cheese variations.

## Steps

1. Add 1 teaspoon salt to boiling water and blanch broccoli for 40–60 seconds, watching for vibrant green.
2. Check the stem texture as the colour turns vivid green and remove the broccoli when it has the crisp-tender bite needed for this dish; do not keep boiling just to improve the colour.
3. Transfer immediately to ice water.
4. Drain and spread out in a colander to remove excess water.

## Reasons

- Brief salted blanching brings out sweetness. Prolonged vigorous boiling dulls the color, makes the texture watery and can intensify bitterness; water-soluble nutrients also enter the cooking water.
- Residual heat keeps cooking broccoli left standing after blanching. Chill immediately, then drain thoroughly to retain a crisp bite and avoid diluting the seasoning.
- Heating and acidic conditions affect green colour retention; vivid colour alone does not guarantee the right texture. Limit unnecessary heating and chill promptly to manage texture rather than adding alkali just to preserve colour.

## Common mistakes

- Prolonged vigorous boiling dulls the color and makes the texture watery.
- Leaving it piled up allows residual cooking; excess water dilutes seasoning.
- Adding baking soda solely for colour can make the tissue too soft; vivid green is not proof of nutrient retention or doneness.

## Limits

The time belongs to this broccoli preparation, not every vegetable. The salt amount does not define a fixed brine concentration.
