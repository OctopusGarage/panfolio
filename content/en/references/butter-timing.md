# When to add butter and how to baste

Match butter to the dish and stage: melting off the heat, spooning over nearly cooked meat and initial browning require different timing.

## Scope

Butter finishes for salmon, asparagus and crispy chicken thighs. Steak searing and mustard cream sauce; browning water-bath chicken breast.

## Steps

1. For salmon and plain asparagus, turn off the heat and melt butter with residual heat.
2. For chicken, melt butter in an exposed area of the pan when the meat is nearly cooked.
3. To baste, tilt the pan and spoon the melted butter mainly over the chicken flesh and edges. Serve sauce beside the chicken to preserve crispy skin.
4. Steak uses a little butter for initial browning, with heat adjusted to avoid burning; its mustard cream sauce takes butter off the heat.
5. Finish the chicken breast water bath and verify center doneness, then dry and brown in butter, skin-side first.

## Reasons

- Butter and seasonings can burn if added too early. Melt butter off the heat, then add soy sauce for aroma; a little lemon balances the richness.
- Add butter only when the chicken is nearly cooked to reduce burning. Baste mainly the flesh and edges. Sauce softens crispy skin; serve it beside the chicken to preserve the crunch.

## Common mistakes

- Butter added too early can burn before the food cooks through.
- Sauce poured over crispy skin softens it.

## Limits

Butter does not always need to be added off the heat; these methods deliberately use different timings.
