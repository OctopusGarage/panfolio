# Covered cooking, reduction and final seasoning

Retain or evaporate liquid to suit the dish, then taste at its final concentration before adding more salt.

## Scope

Managing liquid and seasoning in tomato beef stew, braised aubergine and steak sauce.

## Steps

1. When retaining braising liquid, cover as directed, then lower the heat after bringing it to a boil to maintain gentle bubbling. Monitor the liquid; lower the heat further if it rolls vigorously, or raise it slightly if the required simmer stops.
2. Uncover to reduce, adjust the heat to the sauce and watch its consistency. Keep steak cream sauce over low heat rather than using a fierce boil.
3. Taste near the final consistency before adding more salt. If too concentrated or strongly flavoured, adjust with a little water as directed.

## Reasons

- Evaporation reduces water while salt and other seasonings remain. Final seasoning should account for this concentration; a lid returns some condensed vapour and slows water loss.
- Once a water-based braising liquid approaches boiling at atmospheric pressure, extra heat mainly changes bubbling and evaporation rather than raising its temperature without limit. Heat still matters: adjust it to retain liquid, simmer gently or reduce.

## Common mistakes

- Fully seasoning at the start and then reducing uncovered for a long time can make a dish too salty.
- Using high heat for every reduction ignores the gentle heating required by the cream sauce.

## Limits

Final adjustment does not mean all salt must be added at the end. Keep early meat seasoning and salting onions where their recipes call for them.
