# Caramelizing onions and loosening browned bits

Cook even slices until deeply colored and soft. Pause between stirring for pan contact and dissolve brown deposits with a little water before they burn.

## Scope

Deeply colored, soft and cohesive caramelized onions.

## Steps

1. Slice onions evenly about 2～3 mm thick. Set the pan over medium-low heat, add oil and coat the onions, then add a pinch of salt and cook slowly until softening.
2. Let the onions rest against the pan before stirring, repeating this cycle. Watch them become translucent and soft rather than moving them constantly.
3. When brown deposits appear on the pan bottom, dissolve them with a little water and mix them into the onions. Repeat as deposits form again; do not wait until they blacken.
4. Continue until the onions are deeply caramelized, soft and cohesive. Light brown is an intermediate stage: judge the batch by color and texture rather than stopping when a few pieces first brown.

## Reasons

- Even slices cook consistently; medium-low heat removes moisture gradually and reduces premature burning.
- Constant stirring hinders browning; pauses allow contact with the pan.
- Deglazing returns the rich flavor of properly browned deposits to the onions.

## Common mistakes

- Starting over high heat and burning the surface bitter.
- Stopping at light brown out of fear of burning.
- Mistaking blackened bitter residue for properly browned deposits.

## Limits

For onions intended to develop deep caramelized sweetness; other onion dishes need not reach this stage. Distinguish brown deposits from blackened, bitter residue.
