# Slicing potatoes into even fine strips

Keep the blade upright, spread slices flat in overlapping layers and cut strips with even, square cross-sections; rinse until the water is clear before cooking.

## Scope

Hand-cutting potato strips with a thin Chinese slicing cleaver for dishes requiring even julienne.

## Steps

1. Establish a steady grip: hold the handle with the ring and little fingers, pinch the blade with the thumb and middle finger, and use the index finger for support. Keep the grip balanced and aligned with the cutting force rather than bunching the fingers around the handle and allowing the blade to twist.
2. Cut a small potato slice and place it on the right side of the blade as a spacer to help separate subsequent slices, then continue slicing.
3. Keep the blade perpendicular to the board while making thin slices of even thickness. Spread and overlap the slices as flat as possible instead of building a tall stack.
4. Steady the arranged slices and cut evenly into fine strips with the blade still vertical. Start slowly and evenly before increasing speed.
5. For batch work after gaining confidence, overlap two separately sliced and flattened portions and cut them together. Keep each layer flat and the blade upright.
6. Put the strips in water to reduce discoloration from contact with air. Change the water and rinse away surface starch repeatedly until the water is clear, then prepare for the intended dish.

## Reasons

- An unstable grip lets the blade twist. Keeping it upright for both slicing and shredding makes slice thickness and strip width more consistent, producing even strips with a square cross-section.
- Flat, overlapping slices meet the blade at a more consistent angle. A tall, tilted stack produces uneven strips.
- The potato spacer helps keep subsequent slices from clinging to the blade. Water limits air exposure after cutting; changing it removes surface starch instead of leaving the strips in cloudy starch water.

## Common mistakes

- Chasing speed while tilting the blade makes uneven strips and can bring the edge toward the guiding hand.
- Shredding before flattening the slices, or stacking too many portions as a beginner.
- Leaving freshly cut strips in cloudy water without changing it and rinsing.

## Limits

This covers hand-cutting and water treatment before cooking. Stack portions only when you can control them steadily; soaking is not a long-term preservation method.
