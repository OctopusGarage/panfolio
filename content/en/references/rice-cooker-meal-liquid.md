# Counting liquid in rice-cooker meals

Brown the ingredients first and count their broth as rice-cooking liquid; cook each variation separately.

## Scope

Awei’s rice-cooker variations that transfer ingredients together with their broth.

## Steps

1. Use the amount of water normally used for the same quantity of rice as the starting reference, then prepare the ingredients and broth.
2. Brown and season the ingredients, bring the broth to a boil, transfer everything over the rinsed rice and distribute with chopsticks.
3. Count the broth as cooking liquid, including the milk in the curry chicken variation; do not blindly add a full separate portion of rice water after the broth.
4. Choose one rice preparation for a batch rather than combining all six. Sausage-and-egg rice starts cooking the rice before sausage and egg are added midway; keep that sequence separate from adding every ingredient and its broth at the start.

## Reasons

- Ingredients bring broth, milk or fat; poor liquid control can leave the finished rice too wet.
- Chinese sausage and pork belly render their own fat, so start frying them with only a little oil.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the demonstrated bowl measures as a fixed ratio for every rice quantity, or stacking all six independent preparations into one batch.

## Limits

Adjust for the actual rice quantity, cooker and chosen preparation. Retain the separate midway-topping sequence for sausage-and-egg rice.
