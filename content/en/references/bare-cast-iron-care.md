# Bare cast iron: washing, drying and a thin oil film

Identify seasoned bare cast iron, then wash by hand and dry promptly; apply a very thin oil film when needed rather than leaving a heavy coating.

## Scope

Routine cleaning of seasoned bare cast iron, excluding rust restoration and initial seasoning.

## Steps

1. Confirm the material and let the pan cool enough to handle safely; do not plunge a very hot pan into cold water.
2. Remove food with warm water and a soft brush or sponge. Use a small amount of mild dish soap when needed and rinse well. Do not soak for a long time or put it in a dishwasher.
3. Immediately towel-dry after washing, including the base and handle junction; do not leave it to drip-dry.
4. When an oil touch-up is needed, wipe an extremely thin film of cooking oil over the dry surface, then wipe off the excess with a clean cloth so it is not thick or sticky.

## Reasons

- Seasoning is an attached layer formed by treating oil, not unwashed cooking grease; a small amount of mild soap need not be categorically avoided.
- Prolonged dampness encourages rust, while excess oil can become sticky. Long cooking with acidic ingredients can affect seasoning and flavour, so choose cookware according to its care instructions.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving food residue to avoid washing away seasoning, or soaking after use.
- Aggressively scrubbing with steel wool for routine cleaning, or repeatedly heating thick oil layers until they smoke heavily.
- Applying bare-iron seasoning care to enamelled or nonstick-coated cookware.

## Limits

Follow the specific manufacturer’s instructions. Enamel, nonstick coatings, stainless steel and other materials have different care requirements. This does not treat all iron cookware as identical or cover rust removal and reseasoning.
