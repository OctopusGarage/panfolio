# Measure chicken poaching water and center separately

Measure water temperature to control gentle cooking and the thickest center to check doneness; the final brief sear browns the surface.

## Scope

Whole salt-and-sugar-seasoned, skin-on chicken breast poached before brief searing.

## Steps

1. Trim tendons and pat dry. Rub 300 g chicken breast with 3 g salt and 1.5 g sugar; rest for 10～20 minutes.
2. Boil the water and turn off the heat, then measure to confirm it has cooled to 80～85°C before adding chicken. Poach directly or use a heat-resistant food bag explicitly suitable for cooking. Remove bag air and seal, submerge the meat and keep the bag away from the pot bottom and sides.
3. Begin checking the thickest center after 23～25 minutes and confirm at least 74°C (165°F). If lower, reheat the water to 80～85°C, cook for a few more minutes and check again until it reaches the target.
4. Remove, pat dry and briefly sear in butter over medium heat, skin side first, then turn.

## Reasons

- Water temperature describes the heat around the meat, not the temperature already reached at its thickest center. Thickness, initial temperature and changing water temperature affect center heating.
- Separating gentle cooking from browning avoids a browned exterior with an undercooked center, or excessive heating of the outer layers while waiting for thick meat to cook.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming every pot cools to 80～85°C exactly 30 seconds after switching off boiling water.
- Treating water temperature, poaching time or cut color as proof that the center is cooked.
- Heating an ordinary resealable bag or letting it touch the bottom or sides of the pot.

## Limits

The time is a first checking point for this 300 g breast, not a guarantee for other thicknesses or water quantities. The center check of at least 74°C (165°F) remains necessary.
