# Soy sauce aroma from a hot pan

Finish broccoli, asparagus, mushrooms or fried rice by briefly letting a little soy sauce touch the hot pan before mixing for a toasted aroma.

## Scope

Hot-pan soy aroma for garlic broccoli, seared asparagus, mushrooms and fried rice.

## Steps

1. Once the food has browned and is nearly finished, expose a small hot area of the pan or its edge.
2. Add a little soy sauce to that hot area, let it become aromatic and promptly mix with the food before it scorches.
3. For asparagus, finish the brief steam before seasoning. For mushrooms, evaporate moisture, brown and finish with garlic butter first. For fried rice, cook off excess water before this soy-sauce finish.

## Reasons

- Brief contact between a little soy sauce and the hot pan develops a toasted aroma before it is mixed into the food.
- This is a brief finishing step rather than prolonged cooking in soy sauce. In fried rice, evaporate moisture first and avoid a concentrated splash of liquid onto the grains.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking only of pouring soy sauce onto the food misses the purpose of contact with the hot pan.

## Limits

Not every use of soy sauce calls for a hot pan edge. Salmon uses an off-heat butter-soy finish instead; retain that distinction.

## Comparison note

Salmon is a comparison: its soy sauce goes into butter off the heat rather than onto a hot pan edge.
