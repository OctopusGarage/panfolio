# Thickening with a starch slurry without lumps

Disperse starch in water first, stir it through the hot sauce, and finish when the sauce coats the ingredients evenly.

## Scope

Dishes such as braised aubergine thickened with a starch slurry near the end of cooking.

## Steps

1. Mix the cornflour with water as directed by the recipe; do not sprinkle dry starch directly into the hot sauce. Stir again before adding if it has settled.
2. Once the ingredients are cooked and the sauce is boiling, add the slurry and promptly stir it through the sauce.
3. Finish when the sauce thickens and coats the ingredients evenly. Do not stop heating merely because the slurry has been added, or continue boiling and stirring vigorously.

## Reasons

- Starch swells in the presence of water and heat, thickening the sauce. Dispersing it before heating reduces lumps with a gelatinised outside and dry starch inside.

## Common mistakes

- Adding settled slurry without stirring it again can pour off water first and concentrated starch last.
- Pouring into one spot without stirring can create localised lumps.

## Limits

Starch type, sauce acidity and desired thickness affect quantities and heating behaviour. There is no universal ratio here, and sauce thickening is distinct from coating raw meat.
