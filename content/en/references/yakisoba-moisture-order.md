# Keeping stir-fried noodles toasty rather than soggy

Warm and loosen noodles, brown them separately and cook fillings in sequence; evaporate excess moisture before saucing to avoid soggy clumps.

## Scope

Steamed yakisoba noodles and other stir-fry wheat noodles prepared according to their packaging.

## Steps

1. Lightly warm cold, clumped noodles in a microwave or steamer before gently loosening. Prepare other noodles according to the package and drain.
2. Preheat a wok over roughly medium heat and coat thinly with oil, adjusting for its material. Spread noodles alone across the base, leave until partly browned, turn and remove.
3. Add oil separately as needed. Over medium heat, cook thin pork slices through, follow with onion and carrot, then briefly stir-fry cabbage. Work in batches for larger quantities.
4. Return the noodles. A tiny splash of water can loosen sticking, but evaporate excess moisture afterward.
5. Lower the heat slightly, then add sauce and toss quickly. Keep the noodles chewy inside and able to lift in separate strands.

## Reasons

- Forcing cold noodles apart can break them; warming makes gentle separation easier.
- Vegetables cooked with noodles from the start release moisture that steams them; constant stirring also limits contact with the wok.
- Fillings cook at different rates, so adding them in order controls doneness and moisture. Sauce added last reduces sogginess and avoids prolonged heating that can burn it.

## Common mistakes

- Adding noodles before heating and oiling the wok encourages sticking.
- Making every noodle crisp or letting them scorch: brown only some areas, lower the heat if burning threatens, and move firmly stuck noodles rather than simply waiting.
- Saucing while liquid remains can make noodles absorb water and fall in soggy clumps.

## Limits

This sequence is for stir-fried noodles and does not require every strand to become crisp. Follow the package for cooking or warming each noodle type rather than treating raw noodles exactly like steamed yakisoba.
