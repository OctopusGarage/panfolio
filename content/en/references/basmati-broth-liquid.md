# Basmati rice: count broth and fluff after resting

Count broth toward total liquid, rest covered after simmering and release steam gently.

## Scope

Gao’s basmati white rice and lamb pilaf, cooked covered in a well-sealing pot that conducts heat well.

## Steps

1. The demonstration uses 500 ml basmati and 1000 ml total liquid; the rice measure is volume, not grams. For pilaf, strain remaining lamb broth into a measuring jug before topping up with water. A remainder of about 100 ml is already part of that total.
2. For white rice, level the rice and water, cover and bring to a boil over high heat. Turn to low for 12 minutes, then rest off heat without uncovering for 5 minutes. Do not open and stir during cooking.
3. For lamb pilaf, level the cooked meat, vegetables, rice and measured liquid. Cover and bring to a boil, add raisins, cover again and simmer on low for 15 minutes. Rest off heat for 5 minutes; do not use the white-rice cooking time unchanged.
4. Uncover and turn gently a few times to release steam from the bottom without repeatedly breaking the soft long grains. Finished pilaf has plump grains and no excess water at the bottom; white rice can be covered again after releasing steam to keep warm.

## Reasons

- Broth contributes to cooking liquid; adding a full separate portion of water changes the rice-to-liquid ratio.
- Freshly cooked long grains are fragile; gentle turning releases steam without repeatedly breaking grains.

## Common mistakes

- Failing to count broth toward the liquid total.
- Opening and stirring during simmering, or stirring repeatedly after cooking.

## Limits

Liquid and cooking time depend on rice and cookware; do not apply this example universally.
