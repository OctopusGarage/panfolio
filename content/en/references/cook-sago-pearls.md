# Boiling, resting and checking sago pearls

The 1-minute boil is only the start: small pearls rest covered, while large pearls also need staged reboiling. Check the centers of both.

## Scope

Dry small and large tapioca-starch sago pearls for coconut sago and similar desserts.

## Steps

1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add dry pearls directly, without cold washing or soaking.
2. Boil small pearls for 1 minute, then turn off the heat and cover. Stir and check at about 20 minutes; if white cores remain, continue resting and check at about 30 minutes.
3. Boil large pearls for 1 minute too. Check at cumulative resting times of 30 and 60 minutes; if white cores remain, reboil, turn off the heat and cover. Check the centers again at about 90 minutes.
4. Rinse cooked pearls repeatedly with cool water until no longer sticky and the water is clear and cold, then drain.

## Reasons

- Dry pearls can disintegrate in cold water; boiling water helps the outside gelatinize and set first.
- Cooking through requires heat and time to absorb water. A large volume and a lid retain heat; staged reboiling restores heat as large pearls rest.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the 1-minute boil as the whole preparation, or judging only the transparent outside without checking the core.
- Using the small-pearl resting time for large pearls, or applying cooked-pearl rinsing to dry pearls.

## Limits

These are checkpoints for the demonstrated small and large pearls; judge by center translucency. Do not transfer the method unchanged to other starch pearls or differently formulated products.
