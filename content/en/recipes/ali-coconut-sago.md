# Coconut milk sago dessert

Desserts · ID: ali-coconut-sago

## Choose a preparation

- [Coconut milk sago dessert](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OctopusGarage/panfolio/main/content/en/preparations/ali-coconut-sago/main.md)

## Ingredients

- Dry small and large sago pearls as needed, cooked separately
- 3 tablespoons coconut milk
- 1 tablespoon milk
- 1 tablespoon sweetened condensed milk
- 4 tablespoons cooked sago pearls: 2 tablespoons each of large and small pearls
- Fruit as needed, diced
- Water for cooking the pearls
- One large spoonful of granulated sugar, only when refrigerating the cooked pearls

## Method

### 1. Bring plenty of water to a boil

Bring a large pot of water to a full boil. The generous volume primarily helps retain heat. Add dry pearls directly to boiling water; do not wash or soak them in cold water beforehand, as the starch balls can fall apart.

### 2. Boil and rest the small pearls

Add small pearls to boiling water, boil for 1 minute, then turn off the heat and cover. After about 20 minutes of covered resting, stir and check the centers. If small white cores remain, cover and continue resting. Check again at about 30 minutes for nearly complete translucency. This is a boil-and-rest method; 1 minute is not the total preparation time.

### 3. Reboil and rest the large pearls in stages

Start large pearls in boiling water too. Boil for 1 minute, turn off the heat and cover. At 30 minutes of resting, stir and check. The white cores may still be large and the water has cooled; bring back to a boil, then turn off the heat and cover again. Check at 60 minutes and repeat the boil-and-cover cycle if white cores remain. At about 90 minutes, check and remove once the centers are translucent. The 30, 60 and 90 minutes are cumulative checkpoints during resting; judge the result by the centers.

### 4. Rinse cool and drain

Rinse the cooked pearls several times with cool water to remove surface starch, until they no longer feel sticky and the water is clear and cold. Drain and transfer to a container. This rinsing is for cooked pearls, distinct from avoiding cold-water washing or soaking of dry pearls before cooking.

### 5. Mix with sugar if refrigerating

If not serving immediately, mix one large spoonful of granulated sugar into the drained batch of cooked pearls. Cover with plastic wrap and refrigerate, preferably using within 3 days. Stir briefly after taking them out to loosen them. This is separate storage of the cooked pearls: the sugar is not a fixed addition to every dessert bowl, and the storage period does not apply to a finished dessert mixed with dairy and fruit.

### 6. Mix the coconut base and assemble

Mix 3 tablespoons coconut milk, 1 tablespoon milk and 1 tablespoon sweetened condensed milk in a bowl. Stir in 2 tablespoons each of cooked large and small pearls, then add diced fruit. Mixing the sizes combines the soft chew of small pearls with the springier bite of large ones.

## Tips

- This method concerns tapioca-starch sago pearls. Dry pearls are not ordinary rice and need no preliminary washing. Cold washing or soaking can break them apart; boiling water helps their outer layer gelatinize and set first.
- Cooking pearls through requires both heat and time to absorb water. Keep them covered to retain heat after the brief boil; a translucent surface does not mean the white center is gone.
- Large pearls take longer and their resting water cools, so reboil in stages before continuing the covered rest. Do not apply the small-pearl timing to large pearls or rely only on the timer.
- Rinsing cooked pearls cool, removing surface starch and draining helps reduce sticking. Mixing sugar into pearls intended for refrigeration makes them easier to loosen the next day. Sugar helps separation; it does not replace refrigeration.
- Coconut milk provides coconut aroma, milk adds dairy flavor, and condensed milk adds sweetness and richness. Add fruit when assembling; the dessert needs no mint leaf to be complete.
