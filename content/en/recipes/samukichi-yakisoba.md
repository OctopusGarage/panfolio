# Toasty pork and vegetable yakisoba

Japanese chef series · ID: samukichi-yakisoba

## Choose a preparation

- [Toasty pork and vegetable yakisoba](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OctopusGarage/panfolio/main/content/en/preparations/samukichi-yakisoba/main.md)

## Ingredients

- Japanese steamed yakisoba noodles, 150 g (serves 1)
- Thinly sliced pork shoulder, 70 g
- Cabbage, 70 g
- Onion, 30 g
- Carrot, 20 g
- Cooking oil, as needed
- A little chopped green onion
- A little water, only if needed for sticking
- Sauce: Japanese Worcestershire sauce, 30 ml; oyster sauce, 10 ml; ketchup, 10 ml; soy sauce, 5 ml; sugar, about 4 g

## Method

### 1. Warm and loosen the noodles

Lightly warm the steamed yakisoba noodles in a microwave or steamer, then gently loosen them. Do not force apart cold, clumped noodles. Follow the package directions for warming and preparation. If using fresh chow mein or fresh ramen noodles, prepare according to the package and drain thoroughly. The 150 g refers to steamed yakisoba noodles.

### 2. Mix the sauce

Combine the Japanese Worcestershire sauce, oyster sauce, ketchup, soy sauce and sugar. Western Worcestershire sauce may be sharper and less sweet, so start with less than 30 ml and adjust a little at a time. Store-bought Japanese yakisoba sauce also works; adjust the quantity to its strength.

### 3. Heat the wok and brown the noodles separately

Preheat the wok over roughly medium heat, adjusting for its material, and coat with a thin layer of oil. Add the loosened noodles and spread across the bottom without the meat or vegetables. Leave undisturbed until some noodles develop browned spots, then turn. They do not all need to become crisp; keep the interior chewy. Lower the heat if they threaten to burn; move noodles that stick firmly instead of simply waiting. Remove the browned noodles.

### 4. Cook the pork and vegetables in order

Add more oil as needed and stir-fry the pork over medium heat. Once cooked through, add the onion and carrot. Add cabbage last and stir-fry briefly until the ingredients are cooked while the cabbage retains some crunch. Cook larger quantities in batches to avoid excess liquid collecting in the wok.

### 5. Return the noodles and evaporate moisture

Return the noodles and combine. If they begin sticking, use a tiny splash of water to loosen them, without adding a lot. Let excess moisture evaporate before adding the sauce.

### 6. Lower the heat and add sauce

The sauce burns easily, so reduce the heat slightly before adding it. Toss quickly to coat the noodles evenly. Taste, adjust the seasoning and finish with chopped green onion. The noodles should be toasty and glossy with sauce, lifting in separate strands rather than falling in heavy, soggy clumps.

## Tips

- Forcing cold noodles apart can break them into short pieces. Warm first, then loosen gently.
- Adding noodles before the wok is heated and thinly oiled encourages sticking. Use oil separately as needed for the noodles and other ingredients.
- Vegetables cooked with noodles from the start release moisture that steams them. Constant stirring also reduces contact with the wok; both interfere with browning.
- Pork and vegetables cook at different speeds. Start with pork, follow with onion and carrot, and add cabbage last to control doneness and texture.
- Adding sauce while water remains can make noodles absorb excess moisture and become soggy. Use a little water only when needed to loosen sticking, then evaporate the excess.
- Japanese and Western Worcestershire sauces have different flavors; adjust rather than replacing them in equal amounts without tasting.
