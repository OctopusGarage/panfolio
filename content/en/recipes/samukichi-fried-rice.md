# Garlic, pepper and egg fried rice

Japanese chef series · ID: samukichi-fried-rice

## Choose a preparation

- [Garlic, pepper and egg fried rice](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OctopusGarage/panfolio/main/content/en/preparations/samukichi-fried-rice/main.md)

## Ingredients

- 200 g cooked Japanese short-grain rice, or cooked Calrose or sushi rice
- 1 tablespoon cooking oil
- 1 egg
- 30 g water
- 1 garlic clove
- 1/4 red bell pepper
- 1 small green pepper
- 1 teaspoon salt, added gradually to taste
- A little soy sauce

## Method

### 1. Prepare the rice and vegetables

Use cooled cooked rice or rice with a slightly dry surface, avoiding freshly cooked, wet and sticky rice. Mince the garlic. Seed the peppers, remove the inner white membranes and dice finely so they mix evenly with the grains.

### 2. Heat the pan and cook the egg

Preheat the pan thoroughly, then add 1 tablespoon oil. Use medium heat as the starting point and increase it as needed. Add the egg and lightly cook until it begins to set before adding the rice. Do not premix raw egg with the rice for this method.

### 3. Break up the rice and add water

Immediately break up the rice with a spatula instead of turning whole clumps repeatedly. Add 30 g water and mix to loosen the grains and warm them evenly, then cook off the added moisture.

### 4. Alternate pan contact and mixing

Spread the rice with a spatula so it contacts the pan, dries on the surface and develops a toasted aroma, then mix and repeat. Increase the heat as needed to evaporate moisture. Constant tossing is unnecessary; avoid continually lifting the rice away from the hot surface.

### 5. Add garlic and peppers

Once the grains have separated and excess moisture has evaporated, add the garlic and cook until fragrant. Add the diced peppers and stir-fry until cooked. Keep additions moderate so they do not cool the pan and introduce excessive moisture.

### 6. Season and finish

Sprinkle in the salt gradually and taste, keeping the seasoning simple to preserve the rice and egg aromas. Finally, drizzle a little soy sauce onto an exposed hot edge of the pan. Let it become aromatic, quickly mix it through the rice and serve.

## Tips

- The water in this short-grain rice method first loosens clumps, then evaporates. It is not a method for leaving wet rice to steam for a long time. Once separated, give the grains contact with the hot pan.
- Briefly cooking the egg first lets it begin to set. This method avoids premixing raw egg and rice; that does not make every other fried-rice technique wrong.
- An alternative is to start garlic over low heat to develop its aroma gently. The main sequence here adds garlic after the rice dries; do not perform both sequences.
- Too many additions cool the pan. Relatively dry vegetables or thoroughly cooked meat suit this approach; do not treat raw meat as a ready-to-eat addition in these finishing steps.
- A little soy sauce contacts the hot pan for a toasted aroma before mixing into the rice, avoiding a concentrated splash of liquid directly on the grains. This is not a universal requirement for soy sauce seasoning.
