# Deeply browned roasted cauliflower with a cheese variation

Japanese chef series · ID: samukichi-cauliflower

## Choose a preparation

- [Browned roasted cauliflower](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OctopusGarage/panfolio/main/content/en/preparations/samukichi-cauliflower/basic.md)
- [Cheese roasted cauliflower](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OctopusGarage/panfolio/main/content/en/preparations/samukichi-cauliflower/cheese.md)

## Ingredients

- Cauliflower, 300 g (serves 1–2)
- Garlic oil or olive oil, enough to lightly coat
- Salt, about 2.2 g
- Black pepper, to taste
- A little lemon
- Optional cheese variation: mozzarella, Parmesan and olive oil

## Method

### 1. Cut and wash

Remove the outer leaves and separate the cauliflower into florets. Halve large florets to create a flat surface that can sit against the tray; small florets can remain whole. Wash gently in a bowl of water, changing dirty water and repeating a few times.

### 2. Dry and season

Dry thoroughly, including moisture trapped between the florets. Toss with garlic oil or olive oil until the surface has a light sheen, then add the salt. About 2.2 g of salt for 300 g of cauliflower is a useful guideline.

### 3. Arrange with space

Arrange in a single layer with plenty of space between florets, without overlapping. Place the flat cut sides firmly against the tray.

### 4. Roast in a fully heated oven

Fully preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F) before inserting the tray. Roast for 12 minutes without turning so the flat sides can develop deep browning. Adjust for your oven and floret size; the demonstration uses a home convection oven.

### 5. Check the color before turning

Check the side touching the tray. If it is still pale, leave it unturned and roast for another 1–2 minutes at a time. Once deeply browned, turn just once and roast for about 5 minutes more. The finished cauliflower should have deeply browned cut surfaces, roasted color around the floret edges and a tender center. Serve with black pepper and a little lemon.

### 6. Optional cheese variation

This is a separate finish for the completed basic roast. Top with mozzarella, sprinkle with Parmesan and drizzle with a little olive oil, then broil until the cheese melts.

## Tips

- Large whole florets have little tray contact and can remain pale. Creating a flat side and placing it downward increases direct contact with the tray.
- Wet or crowded florets trap steam and cook more like steamed vegetables. Drying, spacing and fully preheating work together to support browning.
- Roasting without oil can dry the surface without good browning. A thin coating helps transfer heat; the florets do not need to soak in oil.
- Avoid frequent turning to check progress. Brown the cut side before turning once; oven differences and floret size make color a better guide than following the clock alone.
