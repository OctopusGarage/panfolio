# Crispy-skin salmon

Japanese chef series · ID: samukichi-salmon

## Choose a preparation

- [Crispy-skin salmon](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OctopusGarage/panfolio/main/content/en/preparations/samukichi-salmon/main.md)

## Ingredients

- Skin-on salmon, salt and cooking oil
- Butter
- A few drops of soy sauce and lemon juice, optional
- Bonus serving: cooked salmon, rice and soy-milk miso sauce

## Method

### 1. Dry the salmon

Pat the flesh and skin dry with paper towels before cooking.

### 2. Salt ahead

Salt 10 minutes before cooking, then blot away the released moisture. Optionally make 1–2 shallow cuts in the skin; do not cut deeply.

### 3. Preheat and oil the pan

Preheat the frying pan, then add oil and coat the cooking surface.

### 4. Start skin-side down

Place the salmon skin-side down over medium heat. Press gently at first if the skin lifts, then release once it holds its shape.

### 5. Wait before turning

Leave the fish undisturbed until the skin sets, browns and releases naturally. Turn and continue cooking gently until cooked through. 

### 6. Finish off the heat

Once cooked, turn off the heat and melt butter with residual heat. Optionally add a few drops of soy sauce and spoon the butter sauce over the fish. Finish with a little lemon juice if desired.

### 7. Bonus: salmon rice bowl

Flake the cooked salmon over rice and serve with soy-milk miso sauce.

## Tips

- Dry the fish and blot away moisture released after salting to reduce unwanted odor and improve browning. Surface water interferes with searing.
- Salting ahead gives seasoning time to penetrate and draws out excess moisture. Salting just before cooking tends to leave seasoning on the surface.
- Shallow skin cuts help limit shrinkage and improve contact with the pan. Deep cuts let fat escape; scoring is optional.
- Preheating and coating the pan with oil help reduce sticking. Start skin-on fish on its skin side, gently pressing lifted areas only until the skin sets.
- Moving the fish before its surface sets can make it stick and tear. Wait for natural release before turning. Skinless fish should also be left until its surface sets.
- High heat or prolonged cooking drives out moisture and fat, leaving dry flesh. Use gentle medium heat and watch both browning and doneness.
- Butter and seasonings can burn if added too early. Melt butter off the heat, then add soy sauce for aroma; a little lemon balances the richness.
