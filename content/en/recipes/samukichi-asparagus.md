# Seared asparagus and pork rolls

Japanese chef series · ID: samukichi-asparagus

## Choose a preparation

- [Seared asparagus](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OctopusGarage/panfolio/main/content/en/preparations/samukichi-asparagus/basic.md)
- [Asparagus pork rolls](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OctopusGarage/panfolio/main/content/en/preparations/samukichi-asparagus/pork-rolls.md)

## Ingredients

- Basic version: asparagus, cooking oil, salt, a splash of water, a little soy sauce and butter
- Pork rolls (serves 1–2): 3 asparagus spears and 3 thin pork belly slices (about 60–80 g)
- Pork rolls: 1–2 tablespoons garlic oil, 3–5 tablespoons balsamic vinegar, salt and black pepper to taste
- Pork rolls: 5 g butter, optional

## Method

### 1. Peel the tough base

Wash the asparagus and peel only the tough outer layer at the base, retaining the tender core. Do not peel the entire spear or routinely discard a long section.

### 2. Basic version: cut and dry

Cut diagonally to expose a larger surface, then pat thoroughly dry with paper towels. Heat oil in the pan before adding the asparagus.

### 3. Sear over medium heat

Sear over medium heat, rolling occasionally for even cooking. Finish while slightly flexible but still firm rather than limp. 

### 4. Steam briefly

Before seasoning, add a splash of water and steam for about 10 seconds to help cook the center. 

### 5. Season and finish off the heat

Salt at the end. Let a little soy sauce contact the hot pan before tossing with the asparagus. Turn off the heat, add butter and toss as it melts in the residual heat.

### 6. Pork rolls: wrap before cutting

Start with separate, prepped and dried whole asparagus spears. Wrap tightly in thin pork belly slices, then cut into bite-sized pieces. Use the prepared raw spears rather than completing the basic searing method first.

### 7. Sear the rolls in garlic oil

Heat garlic oil until fragrant. Add the rolls and sear over medium heat, turning occasionally until evenly golden, the pork is cooked through and the asparagus center is heated. 

### 8. Glaze and finish

Add balsamic vinegar and reduce slightly while tossing to coat the rolls. Turn off the heat, add black pepper, plate and drizzle over the remaining pan sauce.

## Tips

- The tough fibers are concentrated in the outer layer at the base. Peel them to retain the edible core. Peeling the whole spear weakens flavor, while discarding a long base wastes edible asparagus.
- Diagonal cuts expose more surface and cut through some tough fibers, helping heat reach the center. Ordinary equal-length chunks can leave the tips and thick bases cooking at different rates.
- Wet asparagus steams instead of browning; a cold pan lets moisture escape before searing. Dry the surface and preheat the oil for better aroma.
- High heat can scorch the outside while leaving a tough center. Use medium heat and roll occasionally for even cooking. Finish while slightly flexible rather than limp, allowing for residual heat.
- A brief steam helps heat the center and brightens the color. Searing first and steaming briefly combines aroma with a crisp-tender bite.
- Salt at the end to retain the internal texture. Let soy sauce contact the hot pan before tossing for a toasted aroma. Butter burns easily, so melt it with residual heat after turning off the heat.
- The basic version and rolls are separate preparations, each starting with raw asparagus. Wrap the pork tightly before cutting; its rendered fat flavors the asparagus.
- Sear the rolls over medium heat, turning for even pork browning and heat through the asparagus. Balsamic vinegar balances the rich fat; a slight reduction helps the glaze coat the rolls.
