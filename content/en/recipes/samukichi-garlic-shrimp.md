# Deveining shrimp and garlic butter shrimp

Japanese chef series · ID: samukichi-garlic-shrimp

## Choose a preparation

- [Deveining shrimp and garlic butter shrimp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OctopusGarage/panfolio/main/content/en/preparations/samukichi-garlic-shrimp/main.md)

## Ingredients

- Shrimp
- 15 g garlic oil
- A pinch of sliced chili pepper
- Salt and pepper
- A small splash of white wine or sake
- About 5 g butter
- About 10 g soy sauce

## Method

### 1. Check whether deveining is needed

Check the shrimp's size and the vein along its back. Devein larger shrimp; very small shrimp have fine veins, so avoid damaging their bodies merely to remove them.

### 2. Lift the vein gently

Remove sharp shell points along the back that could prick your fingers. Find a gap near the second or third segment behind the head and bend the shrimp gently to open it. Slide a toothpick or bamboo skewer under the vein and lift. Support it with your fingers and raise it slowly in a vertical direction to remove it in one piece, without tugging hard.

### 3. Rinse and blot

Rinse gently under running water, drain immediately in a colander and blot with paper towels. Avoid prolonged soaking, rubbing or scrubbing with salt.

### 4. Cook in garlic oil

Heat 15 g garlic oil with a pinch of sliced chili pepper. Add the prepared shrimp and season with salt and pepper. Turn and continue cooking so they heat evenly, then add a small splash of white wine or sake for aroma.

### 5. Finish with butter and soy sauce

Add about 5 g butter, then finish with about 10 g soy sauce. Once the shrimp are cooked, plate them and spoon the pan sauce over the top.

## Tips

- Pulling hard by hand can break the vein and damage the flesh. Bend the body to widen the gap, lift with a fine tool and raise the vein slowly.
- A fork or the back of an interdental brush can also help remove veins, though the brush back can slip; toothpicks or bamboo skewers are steadier and easier to control.
- Deveining larger shrimp helps reduce unwanted flavors. Very small shrimp need not always be deveined at the expense of their shape and texture.
- Long soaking makes shrimp watery; rubbing or salt-scrubbing can wash away flavor. Rinse gently, drain immediately and blot dry.
